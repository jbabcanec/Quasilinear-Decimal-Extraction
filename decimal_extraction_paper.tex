\begin{abstract}
We give a deterministic algorithm for a fixed-width decimal window of
$\pi$ starting at position $N$, using $O(N\log^{5/2}N)$ bit operations.
The construction shifts the binary Bailey--Borwein--Plouffe formula and
computes its fractional part without constructing the preceding decimal
digits. Its main ingredient evaluates the resulting exponentially weighted
sum of inverses modulo a power of two. Blocks of length
$N/2^{\sqrt{\log_2 N}}$ have short least common denominators; prime-power
partial fractions recover each block modulo the integers, and a rapidly
convergent real series recovers its integer correction exactly. Balancing
the two reconstruction costs gives the exponent $5/2$. Exact aggregation
of the remaining rational terms, an explicit integer stopping test, and
the known inequality $\mu(\pi)<8$ give certified decimal output within
the same worst-case bound. Stored arithmetic data occupy $O(N)$ bits,
in addition to the workspace of the arithmetic primitives. Numerical
experiments compare the weighted reconstruction with direct binary
splitting, 2-adic Euler summation, historical decimal extractors, and
a certified implementation based on Machin's formula. The smaller
weighted-block bound does not give the fastest measured implementation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Bailey--Borwein--Plouffe (BBP) identity \cite{BBP97}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bbp}
 \pi=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}16^{-k}
 \left(\frac{4}{8k+1}-\frac{2}{8k+4}
       -\frac{1}{8k+5}-\frac{1}{8k+6}\right)
\end{equation}
permits digit extraction in bases compatible with the power $16^{-k}$.
Multiplying it by $10^{N-1}$ also gives an exact starting point for
decimal extraction, but introduces the large factor $5^{N-1}$ and
negative powers of two. The task studied here is to handle those terms
collectively.

Let $N\ge 1$ denote a decimal position, with the first digit after the
decimal point at position $1$. For a positive integer width $w$, the
requested integer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window}
 d_{N,w}=\left\lfloor 10^w\fracpart{10^{N-1}\pi}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad 0\le d_{N,w}<10^w.
\end{equation}
It is displayed with leading zeros to obtain a string of length $w$.
Throughout the complexity statements, $w$ is fixed and $N$ tends to
infinity.

The algorithmic requirement is \emph{no-prefix decimal extraction}:
compute this window without forming $\lfloor10^{N-1}\pi\rfloor$ or
first computing $\pi$ through the requested position and then selecting
its digits. Linear-size auxiliary arithmetic data are allowed. This
requirement is distinct from a polylogarithmic-memory restriction.
Full-prefix algorithms are included below as context and numerical
controls, outside the no-prefix comparison category.

\begin{theorem}[Certified decimal extraction]\label{thm:main}
There is a deterministic algorithm computing $d_{N,w}$ in
$O(N\log^{5/2}N)$ bit operations. It constructs the fractional part of a
shifted finite BBP sum and certifies the output by integer inequalities;
it does not construct the integer part of $10^{N-1}\pi$ or its preceding
decimal digits. The algorithm stores $O(N)$ bits of arithmetic data,
plus the workspace used by arithmetic primitives on $O(N)$-bit operands.
In particular, with linear-workspace primitives its total space is $O(N)$.
\end{theorem}

The theorem uses multiplication in time
$\M(b)=O(b\log(b+2))$ \cite{HvdH21} and the proved irrationality bound
$\mu(\pi)<8$ \cite{ZZ20}. The multiplication time theorem is used as a
time bound; no workspace assertion about its particular implementation
is needed. Section~\ref{sec:model} states the model precisely.

The main arithmetic result is an exact algorithm for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core-intro}
 V=\sum_k \sigma_k 2^{s_k}\mu_k^{-1}\pmod{2^m},
\end{equation}
where the odd denominators lie in a fixed number of arithmetic
progressions, the exponents decrease by four within each progression,
and $m$ and the denominators have order $N$.
The improvement comes from two complementary representations of a
short block. A least common denominator controls its fractional data.
A real hypergeometric series supplies the integer information that
partial fractions modulo $\Z$ do not retain. Both parts are essential
for exponentially weighted sums.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}

Decimal extraction with restricted memory has a substantial history.
Bellard \cite{Bellard97} uses prime-power decompositions in an
arbitrary-base extraction algorithm. Gourdon \cite{Gourdon03} gives a
low-memory subquadratic method and states time--memory tradeoffs.
In particular, Section~4 of his manuscript explicitly discusses a
linear-memory regime with complexity comparable to computing a prefix
by binary splitting, while deferring the detailed construction.
Quasi-linear extraction with linear memory therefore has precedent.
The contribution here is the explicit weighted-block construction
and its $O(N\log^{5/2}N)$ reconstruction and certification bound.
Section~\ref{sec:complexity-comparison} compares the available
time and space bounds.

Modular reconstruction of hypergeometric sums from bounds on reduced
fractions also has precedent in Cheng, Gergel, Kim, and Zima
\cite{CGKZ05}. Here the denominator estimate is applied to short
arithmetic progressions, and an independent real computation supplies
an exactly rounded integer correction. We prove the estimates needed
for every level of the associated trees, rather than inferring a time
bound from the size of the final reduced fraction alone.

Full-prefix computation by the arithmetic--geometric mean has the
bound $O(\M(N)\log N)=O(N\log^2N)$
\cite{Brent76,Salamin76,BZ10}. This concerns a different
computational requirement and is included only for context.
Achieving $O(N\log^2N)$ for the weighted sum
\eqref{eq:core-intro} remains open within the present construction.

\subsection{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} proves the denominator and

\section{Computational model and notation}\label{sec:model}

All logarithms in size and complexity estimates are to base two.
We use $\bits(x)=1+\lceil\log_2(|x|+1)\rceil$ as a size bound for a
signed integer. Changing this convention by an additive constant
does not affect the estimates. For a positive integer $x$,
$\odd(x)=x/2^{v_2(x)}$. Modular residues are least nonnegative
residues unless stated otherwise. Inverses of odd integers modulo
$2^m$ always exist.

We count deterministic bit operations, including the cost of
small-modulus arithmetic and the movement and sorting of records.
Integer multiplication on $b$-bit inputs takes
$\M(b)=O(b\log(b+2))$ time \cite{HvdH21}. Division with remainder
and inversion of an odd integer modulo $2^b$ take $O(\M(b))$ time;
general modular inversion takes $O(\M(b)\log(b+2))$ time
\cite{BZ10}. We use the corresponding regular cost bounds at
smaller precisions. Newton iteration permits geometrically increasing
precision for inversion modulo $2^b$.

Let $\Sarith(b)$ bound the maximum workspace of these primitives on
operands of at most $b$ bits, including their control data. Our space
statements have the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:space-model}
 O(N)+\Sarith(C_1N),
\end{equation}
for a fixed constant $C_1$. They do not infer space bounds from
multiplication time alone. Arithmetic trees are stored as sequential,
length-delimited records, keeping only a constant number of levels.
Section~\ref{sec:routing} supplies sequential sorting and enumeration
procedures, so the analysis does not assume free random access.

For the weighted core, the input consists of a fixed number of
progression descriptions and their shifts, not an explicitly stored
list of $N$ arbitrary denominators. Their entries are generated from
their indices. Fix positive constants $c,C$ and a fixed finite set
of steps $a$, each a power of two at least two. Constants implicit
in $O(\cdot)$ may depend on these parameters and on $w$.
For $N\ge1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 L=\max(1,\lceil\log_2N\rceil),\qquad
 u=\lceil\sqrt L\rceil,\qquad
 \ell=\left\lceil N/2^u\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameter-sizes}
 \ell=N^{1-o(1)},\qquad \ell L^2=o(N),\qquad
 N/\ell=2^{u+O(1)},\qquad \log(N/\ell)=O(u).
\end{equation}
All asymptotic arguments concern sufficiently large $N$. The
algorithms use explicit integer checks for their terminating
rounding procedures, also on smaller inputs.

\section{Denominators and prime-power reconstruction}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

We first record the elementary size estimates used below. For
$x\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-bounds}
 \log\lcm(1,\ldots,\lfloor x\rfloor)=O(x),
 \qquad \sum_{p\le x}\frac1p=O(\log\log(x+2)).
\end{equation}
For completeness, write
$\theta(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\log p$. The product of the primes between
$h$ and $2h$ divides $\binom{2h}{h}$, which is at most $2^{2h}$.
Summing over dyadic intervals gives $\theta(x)=O(x)$.
It follows that
$\sum_{e\ge1}\theta(x^{1/e})=O(x)$, proving the first estimate.
Also, the number of primes up to $x$ is at most
$\sqrt{x}+2\theta(x)/\log x=O(x/\log x)$.
Partial summation gives the second estimate. These bounds require
no prime-distribution hypothesis.

\subsection{A least common denominator for a short progression}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lcm}
Let $A$ be a positive odd integer, let $a$ be a power of two at
least two, and let $t\ge1$. Define
\[
 P=\prod_{j=0}^{t-1}(A+aj),\quad
 D=\lcm_{0\le j<t}(A+aj),\quad
 F=\odd(t!),\quad E=\odd(\lcm(1,\ldots,t)).
\]
Then $F\mid P$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-div}
 D\mid (P/F)E.
\end{equation}
If $A+a(t-1)\le CN$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcm-size}
 \log D\le t\log(CN/t)+O(t).
\end{equation}
In particular, uniformly for $1\le t\le\ell$,
$\log D=O(\ell u)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix an odd prime $p$. Because $a$ is invertible modulo $p^e$, the
number $c_e$ of terms divisible by $p^e$ satisfies
$c_e\ge\lfloor t/p^e\rfloor$. Summing over $e$ proves
$v_p(P)\ge v_p(t!)$, hence $F\mid P$.

Put $d=v_p(D)$ and $h=\lfloor\log_p t\rfloor$. If $h>0$,
every residue class modulo $p^h$ occurs among any $t$ consecutive
indices, so $d\ge h$; the same inequality is immediate when $h=0$.
For $e\le d$, one has $c_e\ge1$. Therefore
\[
 v_p(P)-d=\sum_{e=1}^{d}(c_e-1)
 \ge \sum_{e=1}^{h}\bigl(\lfloor t/p^e\rfloor-1\bigr)
 =v_p(t!)-h.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:lcm-div} at every odd prime; all its
quantities are odd.

Stirling's estimate and $v_2(t!)=O(t)$ give
$\log F=t\log t-O(t)$, while \eqref{eq:prime-bounds} gives
$\log E=O(t)$. Taking logarithms in \eqref{eq:lcm-div}
proves \eqref{eq:lcm-size}. For sufficiently large $N$, the
function $t\log(CN/t)+C_2t$ is increasing on $[1,\ell]$,
for a suitable fixed $C_2$. Its value at $\ell$ is $O(\ell u)$.
This also covers arbitrarily short final blocks.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Partial fractions modulo the integers}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:crt}
Let $\mu_j$ be positive odd integers and $w_j\in\Z$.
For each prime dividing at least one $\mu_j$, choose a prime power
$q_p=p^{e_p}$ with $e_p\ge\max_j v_p(\mu_j)$, and put
$D_*=\prod_p q_p$. Define $c_p\in[0,q_p)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp}
 c_p\equiv
 \sum_{j:p\mid\mu_j}
 \frac{q_p}{p^{v_p(\mu_j)}}\,w_j
 \left(\frac{\mu_j}{p^{v_p(\mu_j)}}\right)^{-1}
 \pmod{q_p}.
\end{equation}
Additional primes not dividing any $\mu_j$ may be included with
$c_p=0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-correction}
 \sum_j\frac{w_j}{\mu_j}
 -\sum_p\frac{c_p}{q_p}\in\Z.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $S=\sum_jw_j/\mu_j$ and $T=\sum_pc_p/q_p$.
Both $D_*S$ and $D_*T$ are integers. For a fixed $p$,
terms with $p\nmid\mu_j$ vanish modulo $q_p$, and for the others
\[
 \frac{D_*}{\mu_j}\equiv
 \frac{D_*}{q_p}\frac{q_p}{p^{v_p(\mu_j)}}
 \left(\frac{\mu_j}{p^{v_p(\mu_j)}}\right)^{-1}
 \pmod{q_p}.
\]
Thus $D_*(S-T)\equiv0\pmod{q_p}$ by \eqref{eq:cp}.
The $q_p$ are pairwise coprime, so $D_*(S-T)$ is a
multiple of $D_*$.
\end{proof}

For a block of length $t\le\ell$, take
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 S(A,a,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\frac{16^{t-1-j}}{A+aj},
 \qquad cN\le A\le A+a(t-1)\le CN.
\end{equation}
We use the \emph{local} maximum powers
$q_p=p^{\max_{j<t}v_p(A+aj)}$. Their product is the $D$
of Lemma~\ref{lem:lcm}. Formula~\eqref{eq:cp} requires only
$O(L)$-bit modular operands: the large weight $16^{t-1-j}$
is evaluated modulo $q_p$.
It gives $T=\sum_pc_p/q_p$ and an unknown integer $z=S-T$.
Determining $z$ is necessary: reconstruction modulo $\Z$ alone
does not determine $S$ modulo a power of two.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prime-tree}
Once the coefficients $c_p$ are known, the rational $T$ for a
block can be computed in $O(\ell uL^2)$ bit operations, with
$O(\ell u)$ bits of tree data.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The total leaf size is
$H=\sum_p\bits(q_p)=O(\log D)=O(\ell u)$, with the empty
case treated as $T=0$. Merge fractions by
\[
 (b_1,d_1),(b_2,d_2)\longmapsto
 (b_1d_2+b_2d_1,d_1d_2).
\]
The denominators are pairwise coprime across the leaves, although
no cancellation is needed in this tree. At a node with $r$ leaves,
the represented value lies in $[0,r)$, so its numerator has at
most the denominator's bit length plus $O(\log(r+1))$ bits.
In a tree balanced by leaf count, the sum of these extra lengths
at any level is $O(H)$. Denominator lengths also sum to $O(H)$.
If the sizes at a level are $b_i$, multiplication at that level
costs
$O(\sum_i b_i\log(b_i+2))=O(H\log(H+2))=O(HL)$.
There are $O(L)$ levels. Keeping only successive levels gives
the asserted data bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Recovering a block's integer correction}\label{sec:real}

The exponentially large value of \eqref{eq:block} prevents
recovering $z$ by individually rounding its summands at a small
fixed precision. Instead we evaluate a transformed real series.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:real-series}
For the block \eqref{eq:block}, define
\[
 S_\infty=16^{t-1}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{16^{-j}}{A+aj}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension}
 0<S_\infty-S\le\frac{1}{15(A+at)}<\frac1{15},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-series}
 S_\infty=\frac{16^t}{15A}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}h_r,\qquad
 h_r=\frac{(-a)^r r!}
 {15^r\prod_{h=1}^{r}(A+ah)}.
\end{equation}
The empty product is one. The series is alternating, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-ratio}
 \left|\frac{h_{r+1}}{h_r}\right|
 =\frac{a(r+1)}{15(A+a(r+1))}<\frac1{15},
 \qquad |h_R|\le\left(\frac{aR}{15A}\right)^R.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Bounding every denominator in the omitted geometric tail below
by $A+at$ gives \eqref{eq:extension}. Also,
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{16^{-j}}{A+aj}
 =\int_0^1\frac{x^{A-1}}{1-x^a/16}\,dx
 =\frac{16}{15}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
 \frac{(-1)^r}{15^r}\int_0^1x^{A-1}(1-x^a)^r\,dx.
\]
The geometric expansions converge uniformly on $[0,1]$.
The substitution $y=x^a$ and the beta integral give
\[
 \int_0^1x^{A-1}(1-x^a)^r\,dx
 =\frac{a^r r!}{A\prod_{h=1}^{r}(A+ah)}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:real-series}; its term ratios and
the product bound give \eqref{eq:real-ratio}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:real-cost}
Uniformly for the blocks \eqref{eq:block}, one can find an
approximation $\widetilde S$ to $S_\infty$ with absolute error
less than $1/64$, using $O(\ell L^3/u)$ bit operations and
$O(\ell L/u+L)$ bits of tree data.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Initially choose the integer
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
 R=\left\lceil\frac{8\ell+16}{u}\right\rceil
\end{equation}
and truncate \eqref{eq:real-series} before $r=R$.
For all sufficiently large $N$, $R\le\ell$ and
\[
 \frac{aR}{15A}
 \le\frac{a\ell}{15cN}
 \le\frac{2a}{15c}\,2^{-u}
 \le 2^{-u/2}.
\]
The alternating-series error is consequently at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-error}
 \frac{16^t}{15A}|h_R|
 \le 2^{4\ell-Ru/2}\le2^{-8}<1/64.
\end{equation}
The count $R$ is allowed to exceed $t$: it belongs to the
infinite transformed series, not the original finite sum.

For an exact binary-splitting implementation, set $p_0=q_0=1$
and $p_r=-ar$, $q_r=15(A+ar)$ for $r\ge1$.
The leaf at $r$ is $(P,Q,U)=(p_r,q_r,p_r)$, and the merge is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-merge}
 P=P_LP_R,\qquad Q=Q_LQ_R,\qquad
 U=U_LQ_R+P_LU_R.
\end{equation}
At the root,
$U/Q=\sum_{r<R}h_r$ and $P/Q=h_{R-1}$, with $Q>0$.
The first-omitted-term test
\begin{equation}\label{eq:real-certificate}
 2^{4t+6}|P|aR<225A(A+aR)Q
\end{equation}
certifies error below $1/64$ solely by integer arithmetic.
If it fails, double $R$ and repeat. Equation~\eqref{eq:R-error}
shows that no doubling is needed for sufficiently large $N$.
For any smaller input, the ratio bound $1/15$ ensures termination.

Each primitive factor has $O(L)$ bits for the asymptotic choice
\eqref{eq:R}. In a node containing $s$ ratios, $P$ and $Q$
have $O(sL)$ bits. Its normalized sum $U/Q$ has absolute value
less than two, so $U$ has the same size bound. Total size at
each level is $O(RL)$, giving $O(RL^2)$ work per level and
$O(RL^3)=O(\ell L^3/u)$ work across $O(L)$ levels.
Multiplication by $16^t/(15A)$ and a division at $g=O(L)$
fractional bits use operands of
$O(RL+\ell+L)=O(RL)$ bits and fit this time bound.
Successive-level storage has size $O(RL)=O(\ell L/u+L)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact block reconstruction]\label{prop:block}
Once its coefficients \eqref{eq:cp} are known, the rational
block $S(A,a,t)$ can be computed exactly in
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-cost}
 O(\ell uL^2+\ell L^3/u)
\end{equation}
bit operations. Its returned numerator and denominator have
$O(\ell u)$ bits.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use Lemmas~\ref{lem:crt} and \ref{lem:prime-tree} to compute
$T=T_{\rm num}/T_{\rm den}$, with denominator $D$; zero
fractions may be retained or omitted. Compute $(P,Q,U)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:real-cost} until \eqref{eq:real-certificate} holds.
Choose an integer $g$ satisfying
$2^g>256(A+a(t-1)+\ell+1)$, and calculate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round-input}
 B=\left\lfloor\frac{2^{4t+g}U}{15AQ}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 C_0=\sum_p\left\lfloor\frac{2^g c_p}{q_p}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
There are at most $A+a(t-1)$ prime fractions. Their accumulated
flooring error is below $1/256$, and the single flooring error
in $B/2^g$ is also below $1/256$. Equations
\eqref{eq:extension} and \eqref{eq:real-certificate} therefore give
\[
 \left|\frac{B-C_0}{2^g}-(S-T)\right|
 <\frac1{15}+\frac1{64}+\frac1{256}+\frac1{256}<\frac12.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:crt}, $S-T$ is an integer. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:z}
 z=\left\lfloor\frac{B-C_0+2^{g-1}}{2^g}\right\rfloor
\end{equation}
is its exact value, including when it is negative.
Return $(T_{\rm num}+zT_{\rm den},T_{\rm den})$.

Since $0<S<16^t$ and $0\le T<CN$, the correction $z$ has
$O(t+L)$ bits. Lemma~\ref{lem:lcm} bounds the denominator
by $O(\ell u)$ bits, and the corrected numerator has that same
uniform bound. There are $O(\ell u)$ prime fractions, so
their fixed-point divisions cost
$O(\ell u\,\M(L))=O(\ell uL^2)$.
The rational tree and the real tree give \eqref{eq:block-cost}.
\end{proof}

\section{The exact weighted core}\label{sec:core}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:core}
Let $m=\Theta(N)$. Suppose the sum
\[
 V=\sum_{\text{families}}\sigma
       \sum_{j=0}^{t_0-1}2^{s_0-4j}(A_0+aj)^{-1}
       \pmod{2^m}
\]
has a fixed number of families, each specified by
$(\sigma,A_0,a,t_0,s_0)$, where $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$,
$a$ is a fixed power of two at least two, $A_0$ is odd,
$cN\le A_0\le A_0+a(t_0-1)\le CN$, and
$0\le s_0-4j<m$ for every included $j$.
Then $V$ can be computed exactly in $O(NL^{5/2})$ bit
operations, with space \eqref{eq:space-model}.
\end{theorem}

Partition each family into consecutive blocks of at most $\ell$
terms. If a block ends at index $h-1$ and has length $t$, let
$s=s_0-4(h-1)$. Its contribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-alignment}
 \sigma 2^s S(A_0+a(h-t),a,t).
\end{equation}
Thus Proposition~\ref{prop:block} applies with a separately
stored outside shift. There are
$B_{\rm tot}=O(N/\ell+1)=2^{u+O(1)}$ blocks.
We now account for constructing their coefficients and assembling
their results.

\subsection{Factoring and routing the small-modulus data}
\label{sec:routing}

Put $B_0=\lceil CN\rceil$. To factor a block, it suffices to
use the primes up to $\sqrt{B_0}$. This base list can be
constructed even by trial division in
$O(N^{3/4}\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit operations: there are
$O(\sqrt N)$ candidates, each requiring at most $O(N^{1/4})$
trial divisors of $O(L)$ bits. This deliberately loose estimate
is below the target.

For each base prime power $p^e\le B_0$, generate the indices
$j\in[0,t)$ satisfying $A+aj\equiv0\pmod{p^e}$.
There is one residue class because $a$ is invertible modulo
$p^e$. A record contains $j,p,e$ and $O(L)$ bits of data.
Over the fixed number of complete denominator progressions,
the total number $I$ of emitted prime-power incidences is
\begin{align}\label{eq:incidences}
 I
 &\le O\left(N\sum_{p\le\sqrt{B_0}}\sum_{e\ge1}p^{-e}
             +\sqrt N L\right) \notag\\
 &=O(N\log L+\sqrt N L).
\end{align}
Indeed, the geometric sums over $e$ are bounded by a constant
multiple of $1/p$; use \eqref{eq:prime-bounds}.
The overhead of scanning the base powers separately for all
blocks is $2^{u+O(1)}\sqrt N\operatorname{polylog}N
=N^{1/2+o(1)}$.

Within a block, sort records by index and prime. A scan recovers
$v_p(A+aj)$, and division removes all recorded small prime
powers. Any remaining factor greater than one is a prime
larger than $\sqrt{B_0}$ and has exponent one.
Sort the resulting factor records by prime to obtain its local
maximum power $q_p$, then merge these maxima into the records.
Formula~\eqref{eq:cp} is evaluated in prime order. This needs
a fixed number of merge sorts and scans, implementable on
sequential tapes.

Sorting the records costs
$O(IL\log(I+2))=O(NL^2\log L)+N^{1/2+o(1)}$ bit operations.
Computing a weight modulo $q_p$ uses $O(L)$ modular
multiplications; computing an inverse uses fast extended gcd.
All operands have $O(L)$ bits. Thus the total small-modulus
work, including the fixed-point fractions in
\eqref{eq:round-input}, is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-cost}
 O\bigl(NL^2(\log L)^2\bigr)+N^{3/4+o(1)}
 =o(NL^{5/2}).
\end{equation}
Small values of $L$ are absorbed by a constant-size convention.
No modular operation is charged unit cost.

At most $O(L)$ prime-power records are generated per denominator.
A block therefore holds $O(\ell L^2)=o(N)$ bits of records,
even allowing $O(L)$ bits of metadata per record.
The base prime list occupies $O(\sqrt N L)=o(N)$ bits.

\subsection{Assembly with linear data storage}\label{sec:forests}

The returned block numerators and denominators have total bit
length $O(Nu)$ across all blocks. Storing every block at once
would exceed the claimed $O(N)$ data bound. Instead, process
consecutive groups of blocks within a family. Accumulate the
quantity
\[
 \bits(\text{numerator})+\bits(\text{denominator})+4t+1
\]
for each block, and flush a group as soon as this total reaches
$m$; also flush at a family boundary. Its last block has
$O(\ell u+\ell+L)=o(N)$ bits, so each group has $O(N)$
bits of input data. The total accumulated quantity over all
families is $O(Nu)$, giving $O(u)$ groups.

Represent a node by $(b,d,s)$ for the value $2^s b/d$.
Merge adjacent nodes by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assembly}
 s=\min(s_1,s_2),\quad
 b=2^{s_1-s}b_1d_2+2^{s_2-s}b_2d_1,\quad d=d_1d_2.
\end{equation}
The sign of the family may be applied at its group root.
At every level, denominator lengths sum to $O(N)$.
The nodes represent disjoint consecutive intervals of a single
progression. The sum of their exponent spans is consequently
$O(N)$, as is the sum of their internal lengths $4t$.
The numerator bit lengths in \eqref{eq:assembly} are bounded
by the denominator lengths, those spans and lengths, and
$O(N)$ additional bits. This proves an $O(N)$ bound for
the whole level, rather than merely for each individual node.

The group tree has depth $O(\log B_{\rm tot})=O(u)$.
It takes $O(\M(N)u)$ time and $O(N)$ bits of tree data.
At its root the odd denominator can be inverted modulo
$2^{m-s}$ in $O(\M(N))$ time. Shift the resulting residue
by $s$, add it to the running value of $V$, and discard
the group. Across $O(u)$ groups this costs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assembly-cost}
 O(u^2\M(N))=O(NLu^2).
\end{equation}
Signed short integers are kept in signed form until reduction,
so a negative leaf does not require a separate full-width
$m$-bit residue.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:core}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:block} and \eqref{eq:block-alignment}
give exact rational blocks, and \eqref{eq:assembly} and the
odd root inversions give exactly $V$. Multiplying the two
local tree costs by $O(N/\ell+1)$ and adding
\eqref{eq:small-cost} and \eqref{eq:assembly-cost} yields
\begin{align}\label{eq:total}
 T_V(N)
 &=O\left(NuL^2+\frac{NL^3}{u}
              +NLu^2+NL^2(\log L)^2\right) \notag\\
 &=O(NL^{5/2}),\qquad u=\lceil\sqrt L\rceil.
\end{align}
The sublinear enumeration overhead has been absorbed.
The two leading costs are balanced at $u=\Theta(\sqrt L)$.
The largest local real tree has
$O(\ell L/u+L)=o(N)$ bits, local factor records have
$o(N)$ bits, and a group and the running residue have $O(N)$
bits. With the arithmetic workspace included, this is
\eqref{eq:space-model}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact aggregation of the remaining rational terms}
\label{sec:word}

Decimal certification will use $\Theta(N)$ guard bits. Adding
$O(N)$ separately rounded fractions to a guard accumulator at
that precision can introduce a quadratic cost. The following
exact aggregation avoids that operation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:word}
Consider $T_0=O(N)$ terms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:word-sum}
 R_0=\sum_t\sigma_t r_t/\mu_t,\qquad
 0\le r_t<\mu_t\le B_0=O(N),\qquad \sigma_t\in\{-1,1\},
\end{equation}
where the odd denominators form a fixed number of arithmetic
progressions with fixed power-of-two steps. Suppose the sign
and residue $(\sigma_t,r_t)$ can be generated from the index
in $O(L\M(L))$ bit operations.
Then $R_0$ can be computed as a rational with $O(N)$-bit
numerator and denominator in
$O(NL^2(\log L)^2)$ bit operations, with space
\eqref{eq:space-model}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Enlarge $B_0$ to $\max(B_0,N)$ if necessary, so that
$B_0=\Theta(N)$. Use the \emph{global} maximum powers
\[
 q_p=p^{\lfloor\log_p B_0\rfloor},\qquad
 D_0=\prod_{\substack{p\le B_0\\p\text{ odd prime}}}q_p
     =\odd(\lcm(1,\ldots,B_0)).
\]
They differ from the local powers used for a weighted block.
By \eqref{eq:prime-bounds}, $\log D_0=O(N)$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:crt} with $w_t=\sigma_t r_t$.
The resulting $T=\sum_p c_p/q_p$ satisfies $R_0-T=z\in\Z$.

Here $|R_0|\le T_0$, so its real approximation is inexpensive.
Choose $2^g>8(T_0+B_0+1)$ and compute
\[
 B=\sum_t\left\lfloor2^g\sigma_t r_t/\mu_t\right\rfloor,
 \qquad C_0=\sum_p\left\lfloor2^gc_p/q_p\right\rfloor.
\]
All divisions have $O(L)$-bit operands, and
$|(B-C_0)/2^g-z|<1/8$. Rounding as in \eqref{eq:z}
recovers $z$ exactly. A rational sum tree then gives $T$,
and adding $z$ gives $R_0=A_0/D_R$, where $D_R$ is odd
and divides $D_0$. Its numerator has $O(N)$ bits because
$|R_0|=O(N)$.

To compute the coefficients, enumerate odd primes up to $B_0$
by a segmented sieve using the base primes up to $\sqrt{B_0}$.
For each prime and each denominator family, its contributing
indices form one arithmetic progression. At an index, compute
$v_p(\mu_t)$ by successive exact division and generate $r_t$
again. Across all primes there are $O(N\log L)$ incidences,
including repeated divisions up to a smaller-order term.
Thus recomputing residues costs
$O(NL^2(\log L)^2)$; storing all residues is unnecessary.
The rational tree has total leaf bit length $O(N)$ and
costs $O(NL^2)$, by the level argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:prime-tree}.

For sequential storage, sieve segments of length
$\lceil\sqrt{B_0}\rceil$ by generating composite-index records,
sorting them, and streaming the unmarked indices. Starting
each base prime's marks at its square preserves the primes
themselves. Across all segments there are $O(N\log L)$
records; sorting costs $O(NL^2\log L)$ and requires only
$O(\sqrt N\operatorname{polylog}N)=o(N)$ resident bits.
Every global $q_p$ exceeds $\sqrt{B_0}$: either
$p>\sqrt{B_0}$, or maximality gives
$q_p>B_0/p\ge\sqrt{B_0}$. Hence the $O(N)$-bit leaf
mass comprises only $O(N/L)$ fractions, including $O(N)$
bits of metadata. Successive tree levels, the sieve,
and the small real accumulator meet \eqref{eq:space-model}.
\end{proof}

\section{From the shifted BBP sum to certified digits}
\label{sec:decimal}

\subsection{An integer cutoff and an exact fractional part}

Write $n=N-1$ and $X=5^n$. For a positive integer guard
parameter $G$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
 K=n+\lceil G/4\rceil+2,\qquad
 P_K=10^n\sum_{k=0}^{K}16^{-k}
 \left(\frac4{8k+1}-\frac2{8k+4}
       -\frac1{8k+5}-\frac1{8k+6}\right).
\end{equation}
Bounding the sum of absolute coefficients by $8$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 |10^n\pi-P_K|
 \le\frac{128}{15}10^n16^{-(K+1)}
 \le\frac{128}{15}2^{-G-12}<2^{-G}.
\end{equation}
This uses only $10^n\le16^n$ and the integer definition
of $K$; no floating-point cutoff controls correctness.

The four summand families can be written as
$\sigma X2^{E_k}/\mu_k$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:families}
 \begin{gathered}
 \mu_k=ak+b,\qquad E_k=n+c_0-4k,\\
 (a,b,c_0,\sigma)\in
 \{(8,1,2,1),(2,1,-1,-1),
   (8,5,0,-1),(4,3,-1,-1)\}.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
These equalities just remove the powers of two from
$8k+4$ and $8k+6$ in \eqref{eq:bbp}.
For $E_k\ge0$ use the residue
$r_k=X2^{E_k}\bmod\mu_k$.
For $E_k=-J_k<0$ use
$r_k=X2^{-J_k}\bmod\mu_k$.
When $\mu_k=1$, set $r_k=0$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bezout}
For a positive odd $\mu$, $J>0$, and integer $X$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bezout}
 \frac{X}{\mu2^J}\equiv
 \frac{X2^{-J}\bmod\mu}{\mu}
 +\frac{X\mu^{-1}\bmod2^J}{2^J}\pmod1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After multiplication by $\mu2^J$, the numerator on the
right is congruent to $X$ modulo $\mu$ and modulo $2^J$.
These moduli are coprime. Its difference from $X$ is
therefore divisible by $\mu2^J$. The case $\mu=1$
uses the convention just stated.
\end{proof}

Let the sums below include all four families and all indices
$0\le k\le K$, with the restriction $E_k<0$ where indicated.
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RV}
 \begin{split}
 R_0&=\sum_k\sigma_k r_k/\mu_k,\qquad
 m=\max_{E_k<0}J_k,\\
 V&=\sum_{E_k<0}\sigma_k2^{m-J_k}\mu_k^{-1}\pmod{2^m},
 \qquad W=XV\bmod2^m .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Multiplication by $2^{m-J_k}$ makes an inverse modulo
$2^m$ agree with the required inverse modulo $2^{J_k}$.
Consequently Lemma~\ref{lem:bezout} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fractional-consolidation}
 P_K\equiv R_0+W/2^m\pmod1 .
\end{equation}
All negative-exponent terms through $K$ are retained.

Compute $R_0=A_0/D_R$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:word}.
Define the exact integers
\begin{equation}\label{eq:UQ}
 Q=D_R2^m,\qquad
 U=\bigl((A_0\bmod D_R)2^m+WD_R\bigr)\bmod Q.
\end{equation}
It follows that $f=U/Q=\fracpart{P_K}$ with $0\le U<Q$.
Only this fractional part is assembled; no integer prefix of
$10^n\pi$ is needed.

\subsection{The stopping test}

Compute the Euclidean quotient and remainder
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decimal-division}
 10^wU=dQ+r,\qquad 0\le r<Q .
\end{equation}
Return the length-$w$ representation of $d$ if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decimal-certificate}
 2^G r>10^w Q
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 2^G(Q-r)>10^w Q.
\end{equation}
Otherwise double $G$ and repeat the computation using the
new cutoff \eqref{eq:cutoff}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:certificate}
Whenever \eqref{eq:decimal-certificate} holds, $d=d_{N,w}$.
For every fixed $N,w$, doubling $G$ eventually makes the
test hold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:decimal-division}, the distances from $f=U/Q$
to the two endpoints of its decimal cell are
$r/(10^wQ)$ and $(Q-r)/(10^wQ)$.
The test states that both exceed $2^{-G}$.
Let $a=\lfloor P_K\rfloor$. Equation~\eqref{eq:tail}
places $10^n\pi-a$ within $2^{-G}$ of $f$.
It is therefore in the same open cell inside $[0,1]$,
proving the asserted digit value and also excluding wraparound
at $0$ or $1$.

For fixed $N,w$, the irrationality of $\pi$ gives a positive
distance between $10^n\pi$ and the discrete set
$10^{-w}\Z$. The error bound \eqref{eq:tail} tends to zero
as $G$ grows, so eventually $f$ is more than $2^{-G}$
from its cell endpoints.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A worst-case bound on the necessary precision}

The proved bound $\mu(\pi)\le7.103205334137\ldots<8$
\cite{ZZ20} implies that, for every sufficiently large
integer $q$ and every integer $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:irrationality}
 |\pi-p/q|>q^{-8}.
\end{equation}
Taking $q=10^{n+w}$ and multiplying by $10^n$ shows that,
for fixed $w$ and sufficiently large $N$, every decimal
cell boundary obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-distance}
 \left|10^n\pi-\frac{p}{10^w}\right|
 >10^{-7n-8w}=:\delta_N .
\end{equation}
Initialize the algorithm with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guard}
 G=32(N+w+1)=32(n+w+2).
\end{equation}
Since $\log_2 10<4$,
\[
 (7n+8w)\log_2 10
 <28n+32w<G-1,
\]
so $2^{1-G}<\delta_N$.
The true value is more than $2\cdot2^{-G}$ from every
cell boundary. By \eqref{eq:tail}, the approximant is
more than $2^{-G}$ from those boundaries. Thus the first
attempt passes \eqref{eq:decimal-certificate} for every
sufficiently large $N$.

The threshold implicit in \eqref{eq:irrationality} is not
an input to the algorithm: its stopping rule is the explicit
integer test. For the finitely many other values of $N$,
Proposition~\ref{prop:certificate} ensures that doubling
terminates. These finitely many terminating computations
do not change the asymptotic worst-case bound. No assertion
about digit distribution, random carries, or a practical
first-pass threshold is needed.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
For fixed $w$ and sufficiently large $N$, the successful
first pass has $G,K,m=\Theta(N)$. Each family with $E_k<0$
begins at $k=n/4+O(1)$, so its odd denominators lie between
$cN$ and $CN$ for fixed positive constants. Its shifts
$m-J_k$ decrease by four and lie in $[0,m)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:core} computes $V$ in $O(NL^{5/2})$ time.

Each $r_k$ can be computed in $O(L\M(L))$ bit operations
by evaluating $5^n$ and the appropriate power or inverse
power of two directly modulo $\mu_k$. The exponents have
$O(L)$ bits. Proposition~\ref{prop:word} therefore computes
$R_0$ in $O(NL^2(\log L)^2)$ time.
The full integer $X=5^n$ used in $W$ has $O(N)$ bits
and can be constructed by repeated squaring in
$O(\M(N))$ time, summing the geometric operand sizes.
The remaining operations in \eqref{eq:RV}--\eqref{eq:UQ}
and the integer certificate use $O(N)$-bit operands
and $O(\M(N))$ time for fixed $w$.

The certificate proves correctness, and the precision
argument proves the claimed worst-case time. All simultaneously
retained rational data, $X$, the modular residue, and the
certificate operands have $O(N)$ bits. Their arithmetic
workspace is precisely the addition in \eqref{eq:space-model}.
For the finitely many smaller $N$, use the same terminating
procedures; enlarging the constants covers their costs.
\end{proof}

\begin{samepage}
\subsection{Algorithm summary}

The full certified construction can be implemented by the
following deterministic sequence.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Set $n=N-1$, $X=5^n$, and $G=32(N+w+1)$.
 \item Set $K=n+\lceil G/4\rceil+2$ and generate the four
 families in \eqref{eq:families} through $K$.
 \item Compute their exact rational contribution $R_0$
 using Proposition~\ref{prop:word}.
 \item Form the negative-exponent core $V$ in \eqref{eq:RV}.
 Use blocks of length at most $\ell$, local prime fractions,
 the real remainder test \eqref{eq:real-certificate}, and
 exact rounding \eqref{eq:z}. Assemble consecutive groups
 as in Section~\ref{sec:forests}.
 \item Compute $U,Q,d,r$ from \eqref{eq:UQ} and
 \eqref{eq:decimal-division}. Return $d$ with leading zeros
 if \eqref{eq:decimal-certificate} holds. Otherwise replace
 $G$ by $2G$ and return to step~2.
\end{enumerate}
\end{samepage}
At each pass use the actual largest denominator in that pass
for the prime bounds and the guard in the word aggregation.
The block guard already uses its actual largest denominator.
Thus the exact block and rounding identities remain valid on
the finite exceptional inputs for which $G$ is enlarged beyond
its initial constant multiple of $N$. Their explicit remainder
tests apply without the asymptotic truncation estimate; only
the first-pass cost for sufficiently large $N$ enters the
asymptotic proof.

Likewise, the BBP analysis gives $O(NL\,\M(L))$ for extraction

provide the same operation in base ten.

For intermediate memory, Gourdon states
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{N^2L^3\log L}
 {m\log^2(N/m)}\right),\qquad N^\epsilon\le m\le N,

cannot be evaluated by substituting $m=N$. Gourdon's prose
instead states complexity comparable to prefix binary
splitting; the detailed construction is deferred in that
manuscript. We therefore retain the stated regime without
assigning it an independently proved exponent.

The dagger in the binary-splitting row refers to the linear
auxiliary-memory implementation documented for y-cruncher's
Chudnovsky and Ramanujan routines \cite{Yee}. An ordinary
unreduced exact product tree can instead store $O(NL)$ bits;
the memory bound is a property of the implementation, not
of the formula alone. Plouffe's 2022 formulas use Bernoulli
or Euler numbers and high-precision root evaluation.
Their paper does not state a complete time--space bound
including the generation of those numbers, so no such
bound is assigned here.

The historical extraction analyses bound the cost of
finite-precision fractional approximations. Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
also accounts for the stopping certificate in
Section~\ref{sec:decimal}. Its $O(NL^{5/2})$ bit bound uses
linear stored data, a larger allowance than the low-memory
decimal methods. Gourdon's linear-memory discussion establishes
a comparable regime without resolving the comparison of explicit
logarithmic exponents. These distinctions limit what can be
concluded from the displayed bounds.

\section{Numerical experiments}\label{sec:implementation}
\clubpenalty=10000
\widowpenalty=10000

We evaluate the weighted reconstruction against two alternative
evaluations of the same modular core and the decimal-extraction
methods of Bellard and Gourdon. A subsequent experiment compares
certified Machin and Euler implementations under a common precision
policy. All extraction implementations compute the requested window
without constructing the prefix. A full-prefix calculation is
reported separately.

\subsection{Implementations}\label{sec:experimental-methods}

The \emph{weighted reconstruction} implements the exact blocks of
Proposition~\ref{prop:block} and their assembly into the core
\eqref{eq:core-intro}. Two C++ comparison implementations use the same
shifted BBP decomposition. In \emph{direct binary splitting}, the
individual rational terms of the core are stored explicitly and
combined by a balanced tree, retaining their powers of two separately
until each merge. In \emph{2-adic Euler summation}, each reversed
denominator progression is first transformed into a more rapidly
convergent 2-adic series, which is then evaluated by binary splitting.

For odd $A$ and $a\in\{2,4,8\}$, the latter transformation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:euler-comparison}
 \sum_{j\ge0}\frac{16^j}{A-aj}
 =-\frac{1}{15A}\sum_{r\ge0}
 \frac{r!(-16a)^r}{15^r\prod_{h=1}^{r}(A-ah)}.
\end{equation}
Newton's finite-difference expansion and
$\sum_{j\ge r}\binom jr z^j=z^r/(1-z)^{r+1}$ give this identity
in the 2-adic topology. All denominators are odd. The $r$-th
transformed term has valuation
$(5+v_2(a))r-s_2(r)$, where $s_2(r)$ is the sum of the binary
digits of $r$; this determines the truncation modulo $2^m$.
Thus the Euler comparison evaluates the core by a different
representation from the weighted reconstruction analyzed above.

The three implementations return twelve decimal digits using a
fixed guard of 192 bits. Weighted blocks and their integer corrections
are evaluated exactly, but the decimal-output calculation uses
fixed-point accumulation rather than the exact aggregation and
adaptive certificate of Sections~\ref{sec:word} and \ref{sec:decimal}.
The timings therefore measure these fixed-guard implementations;
they are not timings of the complete certified algorithm of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\subsection{Experimental setup}\label{sec:performance}

Experiments used an AMD Ryzen 7 5700 workstation with 31.8\,GiB of
memory, running Windows 11 Pro. Computations were single-threaded.
The C and C++ sources were compiled with GCC 16.1.0 at optimization
level O3 with native instruction selection. Multiple-precision
integer arithmetic used GMP 6.3.0; numerical reference calculations
used MPFR 4.2.2.

Bellard's implementation is the author's unchanged Gosper-based C
program, compiled with its long-long arithmetic option
\cite{Bellard97}. The Gourdon comparison is our C++ implementation
of Algorithms~1 and~2 of \cite{Gourdon03}, with $n_0=12$, symmetry
reduction, and a 64-bit fractional accumulator. It evaluates the
low-memory formulation, not the linear-memory regime discussed in
Section~4 of that manuscript. A cached prime sieve increases its
storage beyond the published low-memory bound. Both historical

\subsection{Certified Machin comparison}\label{sec:machin-comparison}

A separate implementation uses the classical identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machin-identity}
 \pi=16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239).
\end{equation}
For completeness, $(5+i)^4=2(239+i)(1+i)$ proves the identity by
comparing arguments. The formula, modular fraction decomposition, and
binary splitting are classical ingredients. We describe their use here
to specify the computation underlying the additional measurements.

Write $n=N-1$. For each $(p,c)=(5,16),(239,4)$, truncate its alternating
arctangent series after index $K$, chosen by the integer inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machin-cutoff}
 p^{2K+3}>2^{G+1}c\,10^n.
\end{equation}
The sum of the two absolute tail errors is then less than $2^{-G}$.
For the first family set $(s,Y,\sigma)=(n,16\cdot2^n,1)$, and for
the second set $(s,Y,\sigma)=(0,4\cdot10^n,-1)$. Its $k$-th term is
\[
 \sigma(-1)^k\frac{Yp^{s-2k-1}}{2k+1}.
\]
Factor $2k+1=p^v u$ with $p\nmid u$, and put $J=2k+1-s+v$.
If $J\le0$, only a fraction with denominator $u$ remains.
If $J>0$, Bezout decomposition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machin-split}
 \frac{Y}{u p^J}\equiv
 \frac{Yp^{-J}\bmod u}{u}
 +\frac{Yu^{-1}\bmod p^J}{p^J}\pmod{\Z}.
\end{equation}
The fraction with denominator $u=1$ is zero modulo $\Z$.
Removing the factor $p^v$ before inversion retains all denominators
divisible by $p$.

The prime-power fractions combine into a single modular core per
family. Put $A=2K+1$, $h=\lfloor\log_p A\rfloor$, $m=A-s+h$,
and, when $m>0$, $R=\min(K+1,\lceil m/2\rceil)$.
Their sum modulo $\Z$ is $W/p^m$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machin-core}
 W\equiv \sigma(-1)^K Y
 \sum_{j=0}^{R-1}
 \frac{(-p^2)^j p^{h-v_p(A-2j)}}
 {(A-2j)/p^{v_p(A-2j)}}\pmod{p^m}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, reversing the index gives
$m-J=2j+h-v_p(A-2j)\ge2j$. Terms omitted at $j\ge R$ therefore
vanish modulo $p^m$, as do terms with $J\le0$.
Every denominator in \eqref{eq:machin-core} is a unit. A balanced
binary-splitting tree evaluates the finite sum modulo $p^m$,
and Newton lifting inverts its unit denominator. If $m\le0$,
there is no prime-power contribution. This computation assembles
modular fractions; it does not evaluate either arctangent as a
real number through the requested decimal position.

The decimal certificate accounts for each rounded fraction.
Let $Q=2^G$, let $T$ be the number of small-denominator roundings,
and let $C$ be the number of rounded core fractions. Flooring each
nonnegative magnitude at scale $Q$ and applying its sign produces
an accumulator $a_0$ reduced into $[0,Q)$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machin-error}
 \left|10^n\pi-z-\frac{a_0}{Q}\right|<\frac{E}{Q},
 \qquad E=T+C+1,
\end{equation}
for some integer $z$. The final unit in $E$ bounds both analytic
tails together. Divide $10^w a_0=dQ+r$, where $0\le r<Q$.
The returned integer is certified if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:machin-certificate}
 r>10^w E,\qquad Q-r>10^w E.
\end{equation}
The interval in \eqref{eq:machin-error} then lies within a single
decimal cell and cannot wrap around $0$ or $1$. Otherwise $G$ is
doubled and the computation repeated. For fixed $N$, the error
bound is $O((N+G)2^{-G})$, so irrationality proves termination
of the mathematical procedure. The native implementation reports
an error if its input or precision limits are reached.

For $G=O(\log N)$, the total number of small-denominator terms is
\[
 \left(\frac{\log 10}{2\log 5}
       +\frac{\log 10}{2\log 239}\right)N+O(\log N)
 =0.92556\ldots\,N+O(\log N).
\]
Cancellation of $5^n$ removes $N/2+O(\log N)$ terms from the
5-adic core, leaving $0.42556\ldots\,N+O(\log N)$ core terms
in total. For comparison, the Euler transformation
\eqref{eq:euler-comparison} uses
$1.29917\ldots\,N+O(\log N)$ core terms in the measured
BBP construction. These are analytic term-count savings.
The conservative binary-splitting time bound for a single attempt
with $G=O(\log N)$ remains $O(N\log^3N)$, with
$O(N\log\log N)$ stored bits plus arithmetic workspace.
They are not the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Moreover, if certification requires $G$ of order $N$,
rounding every small-denominator fraction separately can introduce
quadratic work. The measured program has no exact-word fallback.
Its restricted-guard estimate is therefore not an unconditional
worst-case bound for the complete adaptive procedure.

\section{Further questions}

The two principal costs in \eqref{eq:total} are
$NuL^2$ and $NL^3/u$. Their sum is minimized, up to
constant factors, at $u=\Theta(\sqrt L)$. This explains
the exponent $5/2$ for the present estimates; it is not
a lower bound for the underlying problem.

Reaching $O(N\log^2N)$ for the weighted core would require
an improvement beyond this parameter balance. One possibility
is to evaluate the real correction with less work than the
ordinary $O(RL)$-bit binary-splitting representation.
Another is to share prime-power reconstruction across blocks
while preserving access to their integer corrections.
Substantial cancellation in a final rational fraction alone
does not establish either improvement: the costs and storage
of intermediate computations still have to be bounded.

The extraction proof uses the specific linear denominators
and powers of two in \eqref{eq:families}. Analogous block
identities may apply more broadly, but a certified output
theorem for another constant also requires an appropriate
boundary-separation argument. No general theorem for every
BBP constant or every output base is asserted here.
