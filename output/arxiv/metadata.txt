Abstract:
We give a deterministic algorithm for a fixed-width decimal window of $\pi$ starting at position $N$, using $O(N\log^{5/2}N)$ bit operations. The construction shifts the binary Bailey--Borwein--Plouffe formula and computes its fractional part without constructing the preceding decimal digits. Its main ingredient evaluates the resulting exponentially weighted sum of inverses modulo a power of two. Blocks of length $N/2^{\sqrt{\log_2 N}}$ have short least common denominators; prime-power partial fractions recover each block modulo the integers, and a rapidly convergent real series recovers its integer correction exactly. Balancing the two reconstruction costs gives the exponent $5/2$. Exact aggregation of the remaining rational terms, an explicit integer stopping test, and the known inequality $\mu(\pi)<8$ give certified decimal output within the same worst-case bound. Stored arithmetic data occupy $O(N)$ bits, in addition to the workspace of the arithmetic primitives. Numerical experiments compare the weighted reconstruction with direct binary splitting, 2-adic Euler summation, historical decimal extractors, and a certified implementation based on Machin's formula. The smaller weighted-block bound does not give the fastest measured implementation.
